00 / Prologue

Ansh,
In Draft.

I’m interested in how intelligent systems represent information, how they behave, and what happens when we try to understand or change either.

My recent work spans attacks that propagate through the memory and documents of AI assistants, reinforcement-learning-trained interventions in language-model activations, and the evaluation of visual and speech models. I’m interested in what models encode, how that shapes their behaviour, and how to make increasingly capable systems more reliable and safer.

Selected Work

2026
Submitted research

Share-borne AI Virus

Memory-hopping attacks across LLM agents
Read more

In SPAR research, I designed controlled experiments with independently operated assistants whose users exchange documents. We tested whether adversarial text in one artifact could enter an assistant’s persistent memory, resurface in an unrelated document it later writes, and then be taken up by another assistant. I worked on memory injection, reinfection and multi-hop hand-offs, measuring whether the attack persisted and how far it spread.

Ongoing / preliminary

Sycophancy Is Steerable

Learning behavioural directions with RL
Read more

In an ARENA capstone, I froze LLaMA 3.1 8B-Instruct and trained additive steering vectors in its transformer layers using RLOO-style reinforcement learning. GPT-5 scored generated answers for agreement and flattery, while a KL penalty constrained drift from the unsteered model. Toy objectives helped validate the training loop; early sycophancy runs changed the judged responses. The work remains preliminary, with stronger controls and independent behavioural evaluation still to do.

Multimodal evaluation

ZeroBench

Read more

I co-authored ZeroBench (ICML26), a visual-reasoning benchmark of 100 manually curated questions and 334 diagnostic subquestions. The questions were adversarially filtered against frontier large multimodal models; the paper evaluates 46 models, compares them with a human baseline and analyses where their reasoning breaks down.Visit the project

2025
Speech research

Speech representations

Read more

At Cambridge’s Machine Intelligence Lab, I developed a speech-assessment pipeline for phonological impairment, including phone-level error scoring, articulatory-feature alignment and cohort analysis. I compared WavLM, Whisper and Wav2Vec2 representations with phoneme-level classifiers. Our poster reports an F1 of 0.826 for detecting mispronounced words through articulatory-feature alignment; distinguishing dyslexic participants from controls remained inconclusive on the small study dataset.Read the research

2024—2025
Master’s research

Additive Gaussian processes

Read more

For my Master’s project, I investigated identifiability in additive Gaussian processes: different components can explain overlapping parts of the same signal, making the decomposition hard to interpret. I developed mutual-information regularisation to discourage redundant learned components and encourage a more parsimonious decomposition. This project shaped my interest in what an apparently interpretable model actually represents.
